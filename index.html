The Problem
\n
\n
\n

The ocean is full of ships that don't want to be seen.

\n
\n
\n

AIS \u2014 the Automatic Identification System \u2014 is the global standard for vessel tracking. It is also entirely cooperative. A vessel broadcasts its identity, position, course and speed because the rules say it should. The rules do not enforce themselves.

\n

In Australia's northern approaches \u2014 the Torres Strait, Arafura Sea, the Timor and Coral Seas \u2014 illegal fishing, sanctions evasion, and unregulated transit happen daily. AFMA's Operation Pristis 2026 ran Kimberley to Cape York hunting the vessels that exploit exactly this gap.

\n
\n
\n
\n
\n
i
\n
\n
Switch it off
\n
Disable the transponder. No signal, no track. A vessel running dark can fish protected zones, transfer contraband, or evade enforcement entirely.
\n
\n
\n
\n
ii
\n
\n
Spoof the signal
\n
Broadcast a false identity or fabricated position. AIS-only systems trust the broadcast. Without independent verification, the spoofed track is the only track.
\n
\n
\n
\n
iii
\n
\n
Fly below the threshold
\n
Vessels under 300 GT need not carry AIS at all. Thousands of small fishing vessels operate in Australian waters with zero electronic footprint by design.
\n
\n
\n
\n
\n
8.1M km\u00b2
\n
Australian EEZ. Satellites revisit every few hours. Patrol vessels cover a fraction at any time. The chokepoints \u2014 where every vessel must transit \u2014 are watched by no one persistently.

The Technology
\n

Four layers. One intelligence product.

\n

Ships that turn off their AIS still emit. VHF radio. Marine radar. Satellite phones. Our ground-based receivers capture these emissions and correlate them against AIS to surface dark-vessel intelligence.

\n
\n
\n
i
Layer 01 \u2014 Passive Receive
\n
SDR nodes at the chokepoints
Software-defined radios passively receive VHF, AIS, and radar emissions from vessels within roughly 40 kilometres. No transmitting. No active spectrum use. No licensing complications. RTL-SDR v4 plus a collinear VHF antenna at sites where every vessel must transit.
\n
RTL-SDR v4GNU RadioVHF collinearNo active emissions
\n
\n
\n
ii
Layer 02 \u2014 Core technology
\n
TDOA geolocation
The same emission arrives at three nodes at slightly different times. GCC-PHAT cross-correlation measures those delays to nanosecond precision. Three hyperbolas intersect at the emitter's position. CEP\u2085\u2080 of 1\u20135 km at cluster scale \u2014 accurate enough to cue an asset, persistent enough to matter.
\n
GCC-PHATGPSDO 1 PPSHyperbolic solverCEP\u2085\u2080 1\u20135 km
\n
\n
\n
iii
Layer 03 \u2014 Fusion
\n
AIS & VMS correlation
RF position fixes are compared against AIS and VMS tracks in real time. If RF detects an emitter where AIS shows nothing, it becomes a dark-vessel detection opportunity. AMSA CTS, NOAA AIS, and Global Fishing Watch event streams feed a single normalised schema.
\n
AMSA CTSNOAA AISGFW eventsVMS gates
\n
\n
\n
iv
Layer 04 \u2014 Intelligence
\n
Anomaly engine & cueing
Five detection rules run continuously \u2014 AIS gaps, impossible speed (spoofing), loitering, rendezvous, sensitive zone presence. Each alert carries a 0\u2013100 risk score, a full evidence chain, and a recommended enforcement action. Validated on 34,498 real AMSA CTS rows.
\n

The Solution
\n

We don't beat satellites on breadth. We beat them on persistence.

\n

Satellites give the world a snapshot every few hours. Boobook gives Australia continuous coverage of the channels that matter. The two are complementary \u2014 and we are the only ground layer that exists.

\n
\n
Ground persistence over the channels Australia owns \u2014 that is something no satellite can deliver, and what every Australian customer needs.
\n
\u2014 Boobook thesis
\n
\n
\n
\n
i
\n
Persistent, not periodic
\n
Continuous coverage at the chokepoints. Detection-to-cue latency under thirty seconds instead of hours for satellite revisit.
\n
\n
\n
ii
\n
Sovereign, not allied
\n
Australian infrastructure, Australian data, PROTECTED-capable architecture. No ITAR. No foreign data sovereignty issues.
\n
\n
\n
iii
\n
Independent verification
\n
RF detection of dark vessels where AIS shows nothing. The independent signal that turns AIS analytics into validated enforcement intelligence.
