The technology

Four layers. One intelligence product.

Ships that turn off their AIS still emit radio: VHF radio, marine radar, satellite phones. Our ground-based receivers capture those emissions, timestamp them to nanosecond precision, and correlate them against AIS to surface dark-vessel alerts.

Technical depth

The physics and the maths

The core signal processing is 50 years of validated science. The sovereign advantage is applying it persistently to Australian waters with a software intelligence layer on top.

GCC-PHAT — the timing core
Generalised Cross-Correlation with Phase Transform (Knapp & Carter, 1976 — still the standard). Two receivers capture the same signal. GCC-PHAT cross-correlates them in the frequency domain, whitening the spectrum so the delay peak is sharp even in multipath environments. With GPS-disciplined oscillators providing nanosecond timing, position accuracy of 1–5 km is achievable at 100–500km baselines.
GCC-PHATGPSDO1PPS syncscipy.signal

Hyperbolic positioning
Each TDOA measurement defines a hyperbola of possible positions. Three receivers give two independent hyperbolas — one intersection. Four or more receivers give an overdetermined system solved by weighted least squares, yielding position + uncertainty ellipse (GDOP-aware). The solver handles bad-node rejection and outputs a Circular Error Probable (CEP50).

RF emissions a dark vessel can't suppress
Even with AIS disabled, an operational vessel continuously emits: VHF radio (156–174 MHz, required for distress monitoring), X-band marine radar (9.2–9.5 GHz, required for navigation), and satellite phone (Iridium 1.6GHz, Thuraya 1.5GHz). Suppressing all of these simultaneously is operationally implausible for a working fishing vessel.

Anomaly scoring — the actuarial layer
Five detection rules run continuously: AIS gap (>2h silence), impossible implied speed (>45kn = spoofing indicator), loitering (>2h in 5km radius at <3kn avg = IUU fishing pattern), vessel rendezvous (two vessels <2km apart within 30min), sensitive zone presence. Each alert carries a 0–100 risk score, severity tier, evidence chain, and recommended action.

⚠ Honest technical limits — current state
Ground-based VHF/AIS is line-of-sight limited (~35–50km per node). This is a chokepoint product, not continental coverage.
TDOA requires the same emitter heard by ≥3 nodes simultaneously. Node geometry and overlap zone determines coverage.
Current CEP shown is timing-budget only. Real-world adds multipath and survey error: plan for 1–5km at cluster scale.
Radar detection (X/S-band) requires separate RF front-end hardware — it is Phase 2/3, not validated in current prototype.
AIS anomalies are not proof of illegal behaviour. All alerts are cueing signals for human review, not enforcement actions.
