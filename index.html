Technical depth

The physics and the maths

Fifty years of validated signal processing science. The sovereign advantage is applying it persistently to Australian waters with a software intelligence layer on top.

GCC-PHAT — validated
Generalised Cross-Correlation with Phase Transform
The timing core, first described by Knapp & Carter (1976) and still the standard. Two receivers capture the same signal. GCC-PHAT cross-correlates them in the frequency domain, whitening the spectrum so the delay peak is sharp even in multipath environments. With GPSDO nanosecond timing, position accuracy of 1–5km at 100–500km baselines.
GCC-PHATGPSDO 1PPS syncscipy.signalKnapp & Carter 1976
Hyperbolic positioning — validated
TDOA → position fix with uncertainty
Each TDOA measurement defines a hyperbola of possible emitter positions. Three receivers give two independent hyperbolas — one intersection. Four or more give an overdetermined system solved by weighted least squares. The solver handles bad-node rejection and outputs CEP50. Torres Strait simulation: CEP50 1.24km at 40km baseline.

RF hardware — Phase 2, simulation validated
Emissions a dark vessel cannot suppress
Even with AIS off, an operational vessel continuously emits: VHF radio (156–174 MHz, required for distress monitoring), X-band marine radar (9.2–9.5 GHz, required for navigation), satellite phones (Iridium 1.6GHz, Thuraya 1.5GHz). Suppressing all simultaneously is operationally implausible for a working fishing vessel.

⚠ HONEST TECHNICAL LIMITS — CURRENT STATE
Ground-based VHF/AIS is line-of-sight limited (~35–50km per node). This is a chokepoint product, not continental coverage.
TDOA requires the same emitter heard by ≥3 nodes simultaneously. Node geometry and overlap zone determines actual coverage.
Radar detection (X/S-band) requires separate RF front-end hardware — Phase 2/3, not validated in current prototype.
AIS anomalies are not proof of illegal behaviour. All alerts are cueing signals for human review, not enforcement actions.
